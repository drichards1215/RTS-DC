IThe Statue

On the upper terrace of Meridian Hill Park, Joan of Arc rides into battle in full armor, her sword raised toward the sky. She is the only woman honored with an equestrian statue in Washington.

The bronze is a copy of Paul Dubois's statue outside the cathedral in Reims, France, where Joan saw Charles VII crowned king. It was a gift from the women of France to the women of America and was dedicated on January 6, 1922, traditionally said to be Joan's birthday.

Her sword was stolen in 1978 and was missing for decades before a replacement was finally fitted.

A teenage farm girl who led an army, riding above one of Washington's most beautiful parks.

IIThe Woman

Joan was born around 1412 in Domrémy, a village in northeastern France, during the Hundred Years' War between France and England. As a girl she said she heard the voices of saints telling her to drive out the English and see the French prince Charles crowned.

At 17 she persuaded Charles to let her ride with his army. In 1429 she helped lift the siege of Orléans, earning the name “the Maid of Orléans,” and then escorted Charles to Reims, where he was crowned King Charles VII.

In 1430 she was captured and handed over to the English, who put her on trial for heresy. She was burned at the stake in Rouen in 1431, at about 19. A later church court cleared her name, and in 1920, just two years before this statue was dedicated, she was declared a saint.

IIIVisiting

Meridian Hill Park, also known as Malcolm X Park, is on 16th Street NW between W Street and Euclid Street, about a 15-minute walk from the Columbia Heights or U Street Metro stations. Joan is on the upper terrace, above the park's long cascading fountain.
