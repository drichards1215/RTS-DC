IThe Statue

Partway through the Franklin Delano Roosevelt Memorial, in a niche of red granite, stands Eleanor Roosevelt in a simple coat, hands folded, in front of the emblem of the United Nations. It's a quiet statue in a quiet corner, and easy to walk past.

Neil Estern, who also made the seated statue of FDR, sculpted it for the memorial's opening in 1997. The United Nations emblem behind her honors the work she did after her husband's death. She is the only First Lady depicted in a presidential memorial in Washington.

Of all the figures in the FDR Memorial, she is the one honored for what she did after he was gone.

IIThe Woman

Anna Eleanor Roosevelt was born in New York City in 1884, a niece of Theodore Roosevelt. She was orphaned by age 10 and grew up shy and serious. In 1905 she married her distant cousin Franklin.

As First Lady from 1933 to 1945 she reinvented the role. She held press conferences open only to women reporters, wrote a daily newspaper column called “My Day,” and traveled the country as her husband's eyes and ears. In 1939, when the Daughters of the American Revolution barred singer Marian Anderson from their concert hall, Eleanor resigned from the group and helped arrange Anderson's famous concert at the Lincoln Memorial.

After FDR's death, President Truman named her a delegate to the new United Nations. She chaired the commission that wrote the Universal Declaration of Human Rights, adopted in 1948. Truman called her the “First Lady of the World.” She died in 1962.

IIIVisiting

Her statue is inside the FDR Memorial on the west side of the Tidal Basin, open 24 hours. Walk through the memorial's rooms and you'll find her in a granite niche along the way.
